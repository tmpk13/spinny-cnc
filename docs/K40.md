# K40 on the SKR Pico

A K40 CO2 laser with its M2 Nano unplugged and the SKR Pico in its place,
run with `machines/k40.toml`. Not yet run on hardware. The board side
below is from BTT's SKR Pico V1.0 schematic. The K40 side differs between
units, so find each K40 wire by the label at its terminal or with a meter,
never by position or color. Keep mains unplugged while wiring, and label
the M2's plugs before pulling them: nothing is cut, so the M2 can go back.

## Connections

| K40 | SKR Pico | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24 V + | power input + | from where the M2 got its 24 V, or a separate 24 V supply |
| 24 V - | power input - | |
| supply `G` | board GND | the reference for `L`; already joined when the 24 V comes from the laser supply |
| supply `L`, the wire that went to the M2 | diode anode; diode cathode (band) to FAN3's switched pin | see [Fire output](#fire-output) |
| supply `H` | nothing | it fires on a high level |
| supply `WP`, `IN`, `5V` | nothing; their wires stay as they are | water protect and enable switches, the power knob |
| X motor | X driver socket's motor header | one coil on pins 1-2, the other on 3-4 |
| Y motor | Y driver socket's motor header | as X |
| X endstop signal | X-STOP pin 1 | the left switch |
| Y endstop signal | Y-STOP pin 1 | the back switch |
| endstop ground | X-STOP pin 2 | |
| endstop 5 V | X-STOP pin 3 | one pin feeds both sensors; not for mechanical switches |
| | USB to the computer running spinny-web, or the bridge Pi | |

Left empty: FAN3's rail pin, the Z and E driver sockets, Z-STOP, E-STOP,
PROBE, and the other fan and heater headers. The supply's `5V` never goes
to the board, which makes its own.

## Fire output

FAN3 is a low side MOSFET: pin 1 is the fan rail (the board's input
voltage) and pin 2 the switched side. Its indicator LED and a 10K tie pin
2 to the rail, so with FAN3 off pin 2 sits near 24 V. The diode keeps that
off `L` and still lets FAN3 pull `L` low to fire. A 1N5819 Schottky leaves
`L` at about 0.3 V while firing; a 1N4148 works but leaves about 0.7 V,
less margin. Either blocks the 24 V.

The MOSFET's gate pull-down holds FAN3 off until the firmware runs, so the
beam stays dark through power-on, reset, flashing and BOOTSEL with
`laser_invert` 0. The panel knob on `IN` stays the current ceiling, and the
PWM on `L` sets the power under it.

## Motors and endstops

A motor's two coils: a few ohms between the two wires of one coil, open
between coils. The motor header's pins are 1B, 1A, 2A, 2B in order. A
motor that runs backwards is fixed with `invert` in the machine file, not
by rewiring. On most K40s the X motor and both endstops come through one
ribbon cable, which can carry other lines too: trace each conductor with a
meter and connect only the ones above. A breakout board for the ribbon is
the clean way.

On each STOP header, pin 1 is the signal, pulled up to 3.3 V through 10K
and read through a 74LVC2G34 buffer that takes up to 5.5 V, pin 2 is
ground and pin 3 is 5 V. PROBE has no buffer and takes 3.3 V only.

- Optical sensors: before giving them 5 V, check that each sensor's LED
  has a series resistor on the endstop board. If that resistor was on the
  M2, 5 V straight from pin 3 burns the LED: add 220 to 330 ohm per LED.
- Mechanical switches: normally open, between pin 1 and pin 2 only.

`machines/k40.toml` has the X switch at the left and the Y switch at the
back. If yours sit elsewhere, change `endstop` and `home_pos` there.

## Bring-up

1. Wire everything except `L`, with the panel's laser enable off.
2. Power the board, load `k40` on the Config tab, *Save to flash* and
   restart it: the endstop pins are read at boot.
3. Test the fire output: X-STOP pin 3 (5 V) through 1K and an LED, anode
   toward 5 V, to the diode's anode. The LED must stay dark at idle, held
   in BOOTSEL, across a reset and after `laser off`, and light only during
   `laser S1000 T500`. If it never lights, the diode is backwards or on
   FAN3's rail pin.
4. Remove the test LED and connect `L` to the diode's anode.
5. Jog: X+ moves right and Y+ toward the back. Flip `invert` on an axis
   that goes the wrong way.
6. Block each endstop by hand and watch `?`. Flip `endstop_invert` on one
   that reads backwards.
7. `home`, then measure the travel and the scale (jog 100 mm and measure)
   and put the real values in the file: `full_steps_per_mm` times the
   commanded over the measured distance; it counts no microsteps, so a
   later change of `microsteps` keeps it.
8. With the water running and the knob low, turn the laser enable on and
   test fire: `laser S200 T100`.
